What the model represents

Idealised system

A point-mass bob on a light, inextensible string, fixed pivot and uniform gravitational field. The slider changes the bob’s displayed size and the model mass label; in the ideal simple-pendulum equation, mass does not change the period.

Model assumptions

Read before presenting

The simulation does not include string elasticity, pivot friction detail, air-flow forces, bob shape effects or support flexibility. The displayed period is an estimate; real measurements should be used in the Physics workbench.

Suggested 60-second explanation

What makes this feature useful?

“In industry, a single sensor reading rarely tells the whole story. Our Asset Sentinel compares a machine’s current vibration frequency, vibration level and temperature rise against its own baseline. In this pump example, all three indicators move beyond the demonstration limits, so the software recommends a closer engineering review and lists what should be verified. It does not guess the fault. It makes the evidence and the next checks visible.”

The unique idea is the transparent combination of motion and thermal evidence, with asset-specific thresholds and a saved review trail—not an opaque AI score.

Where this can be useful

Four industrial use cases

The pendulum is a teaching model; the sensor types and structural models change when the idea is transferred to an actual asset.

01 / ROTATING EQUIPMENT
⚙

Pumps, motors & fans

Condition-monitoring teams can trend vibration and temperature on bearings, casings and support structures. A growing vibration level or temperature rise can be a reason to inspect operating conditions and repeat the measurement.

Typical inputsAccelerometer · RTD / thermocouple · RPM / load contextWhat Sentinel addsBaseline comparison across vibration and temperature, with a traceable flag explanation.
02 / CIVIL INFRASTRUCTURE
▤

Bridges & buildings

Structural monitoring uses instrumented measurements to understand how a structure responds to traffic, wind, temperature and other loads. Long-term trends matter more than a single isolated reading.

Typical inputsAccelerometers · strain gauges · tilt / displacement sensorsWhat this project teachesOscillation, period, damping concepts and the need to compare repeated measurements under comparable conditions.
03 / VIBRATION CONTROL
◉

Tuned mass dampers

A tuned mass damper uses an auxiliary mass and damping to reduce a structure’s response near selected vibration conditions. Pendulum-like dampers make the length–period relationship especially easy to demonstrate.

Typical inputsBuilding acceleration · damper travel · structural responseWhat to develop nextA coupled structure–damper model with stiffness, damping, forcing and tuning analysis.
04 / THERMAL SYSTEMS
♨

Heat exchangers & cooling

Cooling equipment and industrial enclosures are evaluated through temperature histories, ambient conditions, flow and load. Comparing a test object’s temperature with its surroundings helps separate ambient warming from a change in the object’s thermal behaviour.

Typical inputsSurface temperature · ambient temperature · airflow / process loadWhat this project teachesHeat-release calculations and why motion or airflow can change cooling behaviour.
A useful operational workflow

How a real monitoring pilot would work

Use a repeatable process rather than treating one unusual reading as proof of damage.

Concept architecture
01Instrument the asset

Choose a suitable sensor, mounting point, axis, range and sampling rate for the asset and expected fault signatures.

02Build a baseline

Capture readings when operating conditions are known and stable. Record speed, load, temperature and sensor metadata.

03Check data quality

Confirm calibration, sensor attachment, sampling, missing data and repeatability before interpreting a trend.

04Compare & trend

Review changes against asset-specific baselines and thresholds over time, not a generic universal score.

05Verify the indication

Repeat the measurement and check operating state, environment and alternative explanations.

06Engineer the action

A qualified person decides whether to inspect, test further, repair, or continue monitoring.

What the app can contribute now

Current prototype
  • Process manually recorded experimental values and calculate derived quantities.
  • Visualise pendulum motion, geometry and a thermal comparison in interactive 3D.
  • Compare a baseline with a later capture using transparent user-defined thresholds.
  • Import structured CSV sensor captures and export a screening report for review.
  • Keep a repeatable record for classroom experiments and proof-of-concept demonstrations.

What an industrial deployment still needs

Required engineering work
  • Calibrated sensors, suitable mounting, known units, sampling rate and documented uncertainty.
  • Asset-specific baselines captured at comparable speed, load and environmental conditions.
  • Signal conditioning, filtering, validated frequency analysis and longer-term trending.
  • Thresholds selected and approved by reliability / structural engineers for each asset type.
  • Alarm governance, cybersecurity, sensor-health checks and a defined human response process.
Convention-ready capability statement. SMART SHM is an educational analysis and condition-screening prototype. The Asset Sentinel uses user-entered or imported readings and transparent demonstration thresholds. It does not identify a specific fault, certify an asset as safe or unsafe, or replace inspection and engineering judgement. A field deployment would require validation and approval for the asset and measurement method.

Convention presentation sequence

  1. Introduce the research question and pendulum setup.
  2. Demonstrate the 3D model and explain its assumptions.
  3. Present measured results for gravity, heat loss and geometry.
  4. Connect each principle to the industry pathway.
  5. Close with limitations, improvements and the team’s Six Hats reflection.

Before presenting

  • Replace sample data with your actual experiment readings.
  • Check units and show how each result was calculated.
  • Explain unusual values rather than hiding them.
  • Distinguish a theoretical model from a measured result.
  • State clearly what the app does and does not do.
